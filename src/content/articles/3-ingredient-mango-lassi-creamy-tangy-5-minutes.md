<p>The first time I made a mango lassi at home, I used too much milk. It was thin, watery, and sad. The mango flavor was barely there. I learned quickly: the secret to a truly great mango lassi isn't just the ingredients, but the <em>ratio</em>. Get that right, and you have a drink that's thick, creamy, and balanced with just the right tang. And you only need three ingredients.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/bbe43bac-bbc1-455d-ab33-78f4fc5ddd2c/0_0.png" alt="Step-by-step: cubed mangoes, yogurt, and milk in a blender before blending" /></figure>


<p>This 3-ingredient mango lassi is my go-to. It takes five minutes, uses a blender, and tastes like it came from a café. The key is a 2:1:1 ratio: two parts mango to one part yogurt to one part milk. That simple formula gives you a lassi that's rich but not heavy, sweet but not cloying. Let me show you exactly how to nail it.</p>

<h2>What Makes a Great Mango Lassi?</h2>

<p>A mango lassi is essentially an Indian-inspired yogurt smoothie. The yogurt provides a distinct tang that cuts through the sweetness of the mango. The milk thins it just enough to make it drinkable. The magic happens when these three elements are perfectly balanced.</p>

<p>The most common mistake is using too much liquid. People add milk or water to make it easier to blend, and end up with a thin, flavorless drink. I keep the ratio at <strong>2 cups mango : 1 cup yogurt : 1/2 cup milk</strong>. This creates a lassi that's thick enough to coat the back of a spoon, but still pours easily. If you prefer a thinner drink, add milk one tablespoon at a time after blending.</p>

<h3>Choosing Your Mango</h3>

<p>The mango is the star. You need ripe, sweet mangoes. Alphonso or Ataulfo (champagne) mangoes are ideal because they're naturally sweet, creamy, and low in fiber. If you use a fibrous mango, the lassi will be stringy. To test ripeness: the mango should yield slightly to gentle pressure and smell fragrant at the stem end. Avoid mangoes that are rock hard or have a sour smell.</p>

<p>If fresh mangoes aren't in season, frozen mango chunks work beautifully. They're often picked at peak ripeness and flash-frozen, so they can be sweeter than out-of-season fresh mangoes. Use them straight from the bag; you won't need ice.</p>

<h3>The Yogurt: Full-Fat is Non-Negotiable</h3>

<p>For the creamiest lassi, use full-fat plain yogurt. Greek yogurt works and adds extra protein, but it will make the lassi thicker. If you use Greek, you may need a splash more milk. Avoid non-fat or low-fat yogurts; they lack the richness needed for a luxurious texture and can make the lassi taste thin and sour. The tang of the yogurt is essential, so don't use flavored or sweetened varieties.</p>

<h2>Step-by-Step: How to Make a 3-Ingredient Mango Lassi</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Prep the mango.</strong> Peel and cube the mango. You should have about 2 cups of cubes. Use a sharp knife and a stable cutting board. The flesh should be bright orange and soft.</li>
<li><strong>Combine in blender.</strong> Add the mango cubes, 1 cup of plain full-fat yogurt, and 1/2 cup of cold whole milk. If your mangoes are not very sweet, add 1-2 tablespoons of honey or sugar. A pinch of ground cardamom is optional but adds a lovely aromatic note.</li>
<li><strong>Blend until smooth.</strong> Start on low speed, then increase to high. Blend for 30-45 seconds. The mixture should be a uniform, bright golden-orange color with no visible chunks. It should look thick and creamy, like a milkshake.</li>
<li><strong>Taste and adjust.</strong> Dip a spoon in and taste. The lassi should be balanced: sweet from the mango, tangy from the yogurt. Add more sweetener if needed, and blend again briefly.</li>
<li><strong>Serve immediately.</strong> Pour into glasses. For a colder drink, blend in a handful of ice cubes for 10 seconds, or serve over ice. The lassi will thicken as it sits. Stir before drinking if you've let it rest.</li>
</ol>

<h2>My Go-To Ratio: 2:1:1</h2>

<p>Here's the specific ratio I use every time, and it never fails:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>2 parts mango</strong> (by volume)</li>
<li><strong>1 part plain full-fat yogurt</strong></li>
<li><strong>1 part milk</strong> (or slightly less, depending on the yogurt's thickness)</li>
</ul>

<p>For two servings, that's 2 cups mango, 1 cup yogurt, and 1/2 cup milk. This ratio gives you a lassi that's thick enough to eat with a spoon but still drinkable. If you want a thinner lassi, add milk a tablespoon at a time until it reaches your desired consistency. If you want a thicker lassi, use frozen mango and reduce the milk to 1/3 cup.</p>

<h2>Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Thin lassi:</strong> You added too much milk or water. Stick to the ratio. If you need more liquid to blend, start with less and add only as needed.</li>
<li><strong>Watery lassi:</strong> You used unripe or watery mangoes. Ripe mangoes have dense, creamy flesh. If your mangoes are watery, the lassi will be too.</li>
<li><strong>Lassi is too sour:</strong> Your yogurt is too tangy, or you didn't add enough sweetener. Use a mild, plain yogurt. If your yogurt is very tart, add a little more honey or sugar.</li>
<li><strong>Lassi is too sweet:</strong> Your mangoes were very ripe and sweet, and you added sweetener. Taste before adding any sugar. Often, ripe mangoes need no extra sweetener at all.</li>
<li><strong>Lassi separates:</strong> This is natural. The yogurt and mango can separate as it sits. Give it a good stir or a quick re-blend before serving.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Make It Your Own: Simple Variations</h2>

<p>Once you have the basic ratio down, you can play. Add a pinch of saffron soaked in a tablespoon of warm milk for a luxurious twist. Swap the milk for coconut milk for a tropical flavor. Add a tablespoon of rose water for a floral note. For a more indulgent drink, use half yogurt and half vanilla ice cream. But start with the classic. It's perfect as is.</p>

<h2>A Final Note on Allergens and Safety</h2>

<p>This recipe contains dairy (yogurt, milk). For a dairy-free version, use full-fat coconut yogurt and a dairy-free milk like oat milk. Always use a stable cutting board and a sharp knife when cutting mangoes. The pit is slippery, so take care. For specific dietary needs or health concerns, please consult a qualified professional.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>

<p>A perfect 3-ingredient mango lassi is all about the ratio. Two parts mango, one part yogurt, and one part milk. Use ripe, sweet mangoes and full-fat yogurt. Blend until smooth. Taste and adjust. That's it. You now have a café-worthy drink in five minutes, at home.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use frozen mango for this recipe?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Frozen mango works perfectly and will make your lassi extra cold and thick without needing ice. Use 2 cups of frozen mango chunks and reduce or omit the ice. You may need an extra splash of milk to help it blend.</p>
<h3>What type of yogurt is best for a mango lassi?</h3>
<p>Full-fat plain yogurt (Greek or regular) gives the creamiest, most authentic texture and tang. Avoid flavored or sweetened yogurts, as they will throw off the balance. For a lighter version, use low-fat yogurt, but expect a thinner consistency.</p>
<h3>How do I make a vegan mango lassi?</h3>
<p>Use a thick, plain coconut yogurt (full-fat for creaminess) and substitute the milk with oat milk or unsweetened almond milk. The flavor will be slightly different, but still delicious. You may need to add a little more sweetener.</p>
<h3>Why is my lassi too thin?</h3>
<p>This usually happens if you add too much liquid or use yogurt with a low fat content. Next time, start with less milk and add only as needed. Also, ensure your mango is ripe and not watery. A frozen mango will help thicken it.</p>
<h3>Can I make this ahead of time?</h3>
<p>You can, but it's best within a few hours. The lassi will thicken and separate in the fridge. Give it a good stir or a quick re-blend before serving. For best texture and flavor, make it fresh.</p>
