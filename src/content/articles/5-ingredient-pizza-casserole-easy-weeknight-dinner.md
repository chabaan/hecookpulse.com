<p>The smell of melted cheese and oregano—that's what hits you when this pizza casserole comes out of the oven. It's the kind of dinner that makes everyone at the table happy, and it uses just five ingredients. No chopping a dozen vegetables, no fancy cheeses, no complicated steps. Just pasta, sauce, cheese, and meat, baked until bubbly.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/743834a8-3707-4fca-a801-f7960e34f2cc/0_2.png" alt="Step-by-step: mixing cooked pasta with marinara sauce and sausage in a large pot." /></figure>


<h2>Why Five Ingredients Work</h2>
<p>I keep the ratio simple: one part pasta, one part sauce, and enough cheese to cover everything. The magic is in the layering. By mixing half the cheese into the pasta before baking, every bite has that creamy, stretchy texture. The remaining cheese on top gets golden and crisp under the oven's heat. That contrast—soft inside, crunchy top—is what makes this casserole feel special without any extra work.</p>

<h2>Choosing Your Pasta</h2>
<p>Short, sturdy shapes are best. Rotini, penne, or rigatoni hold onto the sauce and cheese better than long noodles. Cook the pasta al dente—about one minute less than the package says. It will continue cooking in the oven, and you want it to stay firm, not mushy. Drain it well, too. Excess water dilutes the sauce and makes the casserole soupy.</p>

<h2>The Meat: Sausage vs. Pepperoni</h2>
<p>Both work, but they behave differently. Pre-cooked Italian sausage adds a savory, fennel-scented flavor. Slice it into rounds about a quarter-inch thick. Pepperoni, on the other hand, crisps up on top. I often use a mix: sausage in the casserole, pepperoni on top. That way you get juicy bites throughout and crispy edges on the surface. Either way, the meat is already cooked, so you're just heating it through.</p>

<h2>Building the Casserole</h2>
<p>Use one pot for everything. Cook the pasta, drain it, then stir in the sauce, half the cheese, and the meat right in the same pot. This saves dishes and keeps the pasta hot so the cheese starts melting immediately. Transfer to a 9x13 baking dish (or a 12-inch oven-safe skillet if you want those crispy edges). Spread it even, then top with the remaining mozzarella and Parmesan.</p>

<h3>The Cheese Ratio</h3>
<p>I use two parts mozzarella to one part Parmesan. Mozzarella gives the stretch, Parmesan adds a salty, nutty crust. If you only have mozzarella, that's fine—just add a little extra salt to the sauce. Grate your own cheese if possible; pre-shredded cheese has anti-caking agents that prevent smooth melting.</p>

<h2>Baking for the Perfect Top</h2>
<p>Bake at 400°F for 15 to 20 minutes. You'll know it's ready when the edges are bubbling and the cheese on top is golden brown in spots. For a deeper color, switch the oven to broil for the last two minutes. But watch it closely—broil can go from golden to burnt in seconds. Let the casserole rest for five minutes before serving. This allows the sauce to thicken slightly and the cheese to set, so it doesn't slide off the pasta.</p>

<h2>Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
<p>The biggest issue I see is watery casserole. That comes from two things: not draining the pasta well, or using a sauce that's too thin. If your marinara is on the watery side, simmer it for five minutes before mixing to reduce it. Another mistake is overbaking. Once the cheese is bubbly and browned, pull it out. Extra time dries out the pasta.</p>

<p>This 5-ingredient pizza casserole is the kind of recipe you'll memorize after making it once. It's forgiving, adaptable, and always satisfying. Whether you're feeding a hungry family or just want leftovers for the week, it delivers on comfort without the hassle.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use any type of pasta?</h3>
<p>Yes, short pasta shapes work best—rotini, penne, rigatoni, or fusilli hold the sauce well. Avoid long pasta like spaghetti. Cook to al dente so it doesn't get mushy in the oven.</p>
<h3>How do I make this casserole gluten-free?</h3>
<p>Simply swap the pasta for your favorite gluten-free short pasta. Check that the marinara sauce and sausage are also gluten-free (most are, but verify labels). The cheese is naturally gluten-free.</p>
<h3>Can I add vegetables?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Stir in 1 cup of chopped bell peppers, mushrooms, or spinach along with the sauce. Sauté mushrooms first to remove excess moisture. This keeps the casserole from becoming watery.</p>
<h3>What if I don't have an oven-safe skillet?</h3>
<p>No problem. Use a 9x13 baking dish instead. After mixing everything in a pot, transfer it to the dish and bake. Just note that a skillet gives nicer browning on the edges.</p>
<h3>My cheese burned on top but the inside was cold—what went wrong?</h3>
<p>This happens if the oven is too hot or the dish is too full. Bake at 400°F, and if your casserole is very deep, cover with foil for the first 15 minutes, then uncover to brown. Always let it rest 5 minutes after baking to even out the temperature.</p>
