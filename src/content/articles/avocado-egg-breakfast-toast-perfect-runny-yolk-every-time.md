<p>There's a moment when you cut into a perfectly fried egg on avocado toast. The yolk spills slowly, pooling into the crevices of creamy green mash, soaking the crispy edges of the bread. That first bite—warm, rich, bright—is what this recipe is all about.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/031f71ec-2c0a-4094-8682-0a6837f99dab/0_3.png" alt="Step showing an egg frying in a non-stick skillet with a runny yolk and set white" /></figure>


<p>I've tested a lot of methods to get that yolk just right. Not too runny, not too set. The avocado should be creamy, not watery. And the bread? It needs to hold up without turning into a sad, soggy mess. Here's exactly how to do it.</p>

<h2>The Bread: Your Foundation</h2>
<p>Start with a slice of hearty bread. Sourdough or a dense whole grain works best. Thin sandwich bread will collapse under the weight of the toppings. Toast it until it's golden and firm—about 5-7 minutes in a 375°F oven, or on the highest setting in your toaster. The goal is a surface that can support the avocado and egg without bending. A common mistake is under-toasting. If the bread is still soft in the center, it will turn soggy within seconds. You want a crisp shell and a chewy interior.</p>

<h2>The Avocado: Texture and Timing</h2>
<p>Choose a ripe avocado. It should yield slightly to gentle pressure at the stem end. Cut it in half lengthwise, twist to separate, and remove the pit. Scoop the flesh into a small bowl. Mash with a fork—leave some small chunks for texture. Season with a pinch of flaky salt. I keep the ratio simple: one half avocado per slice of bread. A squeeze of lemon or lime juice adds brightness and helps slow browning, but it's optional. Do not over-mash; a paste-like consistency loses the pleasant contrast of creamy and chunky. Spread the mash evenly over the toast, leaving a slight indent in the center for the egg to nestle into.</p>

<h2>The Egg: Technique for a Runny Yolk</h2>
<p>Heat a small non-stick skillet over medium-low heat. Add a teaspoon of olive oil or butter—enough to coat the pan. Crack the egg into a small bowl first. This prevents shell bits from ending up in the pan and lets you slide the egg in gently. Cook for 2-3 minutes. Watch the white: it should be fully set with no translucent areas, but the yolk should still wobble when you shake the pan. The edges will be lacy and lightly browned. Do not flip. Flipping will cook the yolk through, and you'll lose that beautiful runny center. If you prefer a firmer yolk, cook for 4 minutes, but the magic of this dish is in the jammy yolk that acts as a sauce.</p>

<h3>Common Mistake: High Heat</h3>
<p>Cranking the heat to speed things up will give you burnt edges and a raw white. Low and slow is the rule. The egg white needs time to coagulate without the yolk setting. Patience pays off.</p>

<h2>Assembling the Toast</h2>
<p>Slide the spatula under the egg carefully—the yolk is fragile. Place it directly onto the avocado bed. Season immediately with flaky sea salt and freshly ground black pepper. A pinch of red pepper flakes adds a gentle heat that cuts through the richness. Serve at once. The toast waits for no one.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>This avocado egg breakfast toast comes together in under 10 minutes. The key is a sturdy toast, a well-mashed avocado, and an egg cooked low and slow until the white is set and the yolk is runny. No fancy equipment, no complicated steps. Just a breakfast that actually keeps you full and tastes like you put in effort.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>What type of bread works best for avocado egg toast?</h3>
<p>A hearty bread like sourdough or whole grain holds up best. It stays crisp under the avocado and egg without getting soggy.</p>
<h3>How do I keep the avocado from turning brown?</h3>
<p>Mash the avocado just before serving. If prepping ahead, press plastic wrap directly onto the surface of the mash to minimize air exposure. A squeeze of lemon or lime juice also helps.</p>
<h3>Can I use a poached egg instead of fried?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Poach the egg in simmering water with a splash of vinegar for 3-4 minutes until the white is set but the yolk is runny. Drain well before placing on the avocado.</p>
<h3>Is this recipe suitable for meal prep?</h3>
<p>Not really—the toast is best fresh. You can prep the mashed avocado (with plastic wrap pressed on top) and store it in the fridge for up to a day, but fry the egg just before eating.</p>
