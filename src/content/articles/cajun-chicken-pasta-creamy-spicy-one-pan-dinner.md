<p>The first thing you notice is the sizzle. Chicken hits a hot skillet, and that smoky, peppery aroma of Cajun seasoning fills the kitchen. A few minutes later, cream and Parmesan melt into a sauce that clings to every piece of pasta. This is not a complicated dish. It is a reliable one-pan dinner that comes together in under 30 minutes, with ingredients you can find at any grocery store.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/92488aaa-b692-4d3a-969c-25551c4c55fe/0_3.png" alt="Step showing raw chicken breasts seasoned with Cajun spice in a hot skillet with oil" /></figure>


<p>I keep the ratio simple: 8 ounces of pasta to 1 cup of cream and ½ cup of broth. That gives you a sauce that coats without being heavy. The pasta cooks right in the sauce, absorbing flavor as it softens. No separate pot. No draining. One skillet, and you are done.</p>

<h2>The Right Cajun Seasoning</h2>
<p>Store-bought Cajun blends vary wildly. Some are heavy on salt, others on cayenne. I prefer a mix with paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, oregano, thyme, and just enough cayenne for warmth. If your blend has salt, reduce the added salt in the recipe. Taste the sauce before adding Parmesan—cheese adds its own salt.</p>

<h3>How to Season the Chicken</h3>
<p>Pat the chicken dry first. That step matters. Moisture on the surface steams instead of sears, and you lose that golden crust. Sprinkle one teaspoon of Cajun seasoning over both sides, plus a pinch of salt and pepper. Let it sit while you prep the vegetables—five minutes is enough for the seasoning to adhere.</p>

<h2>Building the Sauce</h2>
<p>After the chicken rests, the skillet holds browned bits called fond. That is flavor. When you add onion and bell pepper, scrape them up with a wooden spoon as they cook. The moisture from the vegetables loosens the fond and blends it into the sauce.</p>

<p>Heavy cream gives the richest texture, but half-and-half works if you prefer less richness. Chicken broth thins the sauce just enough for the pasta to cook evenly. I add the remaining teaspoon of Cajun seasoning here—not earlier—so the spice blooms in the cream.</p>

<h3>The Pasta Trick</h3>
<p>Dump the dry pasta straight into the sauce. Do not cook it first. Stir once to coat, then cover the skillet. Simmer gently for 12 to 14 minutes, stirring halfway through. The pasta releases starch as it cooks, which thickens the sauce naturally. If the sauce looks too thick before the pasta is tender, add a splash of broth or water. If it is too thin, uncover and simmer an extra minute or two.</p>

<h2>Finishing Touches</h2>
<p>Once the pasta is tender, remove the skillet from heat. Stir in Parmesan and butter off the burner—high heat can make the cheese clump. The butter adds a silky finish. Slice the rested chicken into strips and toss everything together. The juices from the chicken add another layer of flavor.</p>

<p>Look for a sauce that clings to the pasta without pooling at the bottom of the bowl. The chicken should be juicy, not dry. If you cut into a piece and it looks chalky, it cooked too long. Next time, take the chicken off the heat at 160°F—carryover cooking will bring it to 165°F.</p>

<h2>Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Overcrowding the pan:</strong> Cook the chicken in a single layer with space around each piece. If it steams instead of sears, you lose the crust.</li>
<li><strong>Skipping the rest:</strong> Let the chicken rest for five minutes after cooking. Slicing too early releases juices and dries out the meat.</li>
<li><strong>Boiling the cream:</strong> Keep the sauce at a gentle simmer. A rolling boil can cause the cream to separate.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>This Cajun chicken pasta is a weeknight win because it asks for one pan, simple ingredients, and a little patience with timing. The technique is straightforward: sear, soften, simmer, finish. That is all. The result is a creamy, spicy dinner that feels like more effort than it really is.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use pre-cooked chicken?</h3>
<p>Yes, but the flavor will be less intense. Add cooked chicken at the end to just warm through, about 2 minutes. The searing step adds depth to the sauce.</p>
<h3>What pasta shape works best?</h3>
<p>Short pasta like penne, rigatoni, or fusilli work best because they hold the sauce well. Avoid long strands like spaghetti—they don't cook evenly in the sauce.</p>
<h3>How do I make this less spicy?</h3>
<p>Use a mild Cajun seasoning or reduce it to 1 teaspoon total. You can also add an extra tablespoon of cream to tone down heat.</p>
<h3>Can I add vegetables?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Add 1 cup sliced mushrooms or zucchini with the onion and bell pepper. Cook until softened before adding garlic.</p>
