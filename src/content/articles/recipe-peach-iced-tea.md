The syrup is shelf-stable for a day without refrigeration if stored in a sealed jar, and it takes up less cooler space than a gallon of pre-made tea. Plus, you can adjust the sweetness per glass for picky kids or low-sugar campers.</p>\n\n<h3>My Ratio for Balanced Peach Tea</h3>\n<p>I keep the ratio at <strong>4 cups water to 4 tea bags to 1 cup peach slices to 1/2 cup sugar</strong>. This yields a syrup that, when diluted with an equal amount of cold water, has a gentle tea flavor and a peachy aroma. <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use frozen peaches for peach iced tea?</h3>
<p>Yes, frozen sliced peaches work well. Thaw them first, then follow the same simmering method. They often release more juice, so you may get a slightly stronger peach flavor.</p>
<h3>How do I keep my peach iced tea from turning bitter?</h3>
<p>Bitter tea comes from over-steeping or using water that's too hot. Steep black tea for exactly 5 minutes at a full boil. If you prefer a milder tea, steep for 3-4 minutes. Remove the tea bags promptly.</p>
<h3>Can I make this with green tea or herbal tea?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. For green tea, use water at 175°F (80°C) and steep for 2-3 minutes. For herbal teas like peach or chamomile, steep for 5-7 minutes. Adjust sweetener to taste.</p>
<h3>How long does homemade peach iced tea last in the fridge?</h3>
<p>It keeps well for up to 4 days in a sealed container. The peach flavor may intensify over time. If you add fresh mint, remove it after 24 hours to prevent bitterness.</p>
