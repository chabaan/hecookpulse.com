<p>The first time I made this salad, it was the color that hooked me. That brilliant, grassy green of fresh spring peas, punctuated by white feta and the occasional pink radish. One bite, and the sweetness of the peas paired with the salty, creamy feta made it an instant favorite.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/d8cb99d2-5685-47ef-a88a-0d73f75aa9c0/0_2.png" alt="Step showing blanched peas being transferred to ice water to stop cooking." /></figure>
<p>This Spring Pea &amp; Feta Salad is less a recipe and more a method for capturing the season at its peak. The technique is simple, but a few small choices make the difference between a good salad and a great one.</p><h2>Why This Salad Works</h2><p>Peas are naturally sweet, but that sweetness needs a counterpoint. Salty feta provides it. Fresh mint adds a cool, aromatic lift. And a bright lemon dressing ties everything together without overwhelming the delicate pea flavor.</p><p>The real trick is in how you handle the peas. Overcook them, and they turn dull and mealy. Treat them right, and they pop with sweetness and color.</p><h2>Choosing Your Peas</h2><p>Fresh spring peas are wonderful if you can find them. Look for pods that are firm, bright green, and snap when bent. Shell them just before using. But honestly, frozen peas are often better for this salad. They are picked at peak ripeness and flash-frozen, so their sugar content is locked in. I use frozen peas more often than not, and the salad is always vibrant.</p><h2>The Blanching Method (For Fresh Peas)</h2><p>If you use fresh peas, blanch them briefly. Bring a medium saucepan of generously salted water to a rolling boil. Drop in the shelled peas and set a timer for exactly 1 minute for small peas, 2 minutes for larger ones. They should turn bright green and taste just tender.</p><p>Immediately drain them and plunge them into a bowl of ice water. This stops the cooking instantly and sets the color. Let them sit for at least 2 minutes, then drain again and pat dry with a clean kitchen towel. Any residual water will dilute the dressing.</p><p><strong>Common mistake:</strong> Skipping the ice bath. The residual heat will continue to cook the peas, leaving them soft and faded.</p><h2>Handling Frozen Peas</h2><p>For frozen peas, do not cook them. Simply place them in a colander and run cool tap water over them for about 30 seconds. Shake off the excess water and let them drain for a minute. That is all they need. They will be sweet, tender, and perfectly textured.</p><h2>The Dressing: Simple and Balanced</h2><p>I use a 3:1 ratio of oil to acid here — 3 tablespoons olive oil to 1 tablespoon lemon juice. That keeps the dressing bright but not sharp. A little Dijon mustard helps emulsify the dressing and adds a subtle warmth. Lemon zest is not optional; it adds a fragrant, citrusy note that juice alone cannot provide.</p><p>Whisk the dressing in a small bowl or shake it in a jar until it is thick and creamy. Taste it on a pea before adding salt — feta is salty, so you may not need much.</p><h2>Assembling the Salad</h2><p>Combine the peas, crumbled feta, and chopped mint in a large bowl. If you are using radishes, slice them paper-thin — a mandoline works well here — and add them last. Pour the dressing over the top and toss gently with your hands or a rubber spatula. Over-tossing will break up the feta.</p><p>Let the salad sit for 5 minutes before serving. This allows the flavors to meld. Serve at room temperature or slightly chilled.</p><h2>Make-Ahead Tips</h2><p>This salad is best within a few hours of assembling. If you need to prep ahead, keep the dressing separate and toss just before serving. The peas and mint can be prepped a day in advance and stored in the fridge in a sealed container. Feta can be crumbled and refrigerated separately.</p><h2>Variations to Try</h2><ul><li><strong>Add crunch:</strong> Toasted pine nuts, slivered almonds, or sunflower seeds.</li><li><strong>Add protein:</strong> Cooked chickpeas, grilled shrimp, or shredded rotisserie chicken.</li><li><strong>Change the herb:</strong> Dill or basil work beautifully instead of mint.</li><li><strong>Make it dairy-free:</strong> Omit the feta and add diced avocado for creaminess.</li></ul><h2>Takeaway</h2><p>This Spring Pea &amp; Feta Salad is proof that simple ingredients, handled with care, create something memorable. The key is in the pea treatment — quick blanch or gentle thaw — and a dressing that respects rather than masks the main ingredient. Make it once, and you will find yourself reaching for peas long after spring is gone.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use frozen peas instead of fresh?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Frozen peas are actually ideal here because they are picked and flash-frozen at peak sweetness. Just thaw them by running under cool water in a colander for 30 seconds, then drain well. No need to cook them further.</p>
<h3>How do I keep the peas from getting mushy?</h3>
<p>The key is to not overcook them. If using fresh peas, blanch for only 1-2 minutes, then immediately plunge into ice water. For frozen peas, just thaw gently — no cooking. Overcooking causes the skins to wrinkle and the insides to turn mealy.</p>
<h3>Can I make this salad ahead of time?</h3>
<p>Yes, but with a caveat. The dressing will soften the peas and mint over time. For best results, prep the peas, feta, and mint separately, and store the dressing in a jar. Toss everything together no more than 2 hours before serving.</p>
<h3>What can I add for more texture or protein?</h3>
<p>Sliced radishes add a lovely crunch. For protein, try cooked chickpeas, grilled chicken, or toasted pine nuts. Toasted almonds or walnuts also work well.</p>
<h3>Is this salad gluten-free?</h3>
<p>Yes, all the ingredients listed are naturally gluten-free. Just double-check that your Dijon mustard is certified gluten-free if you have celiac disease.</p>
